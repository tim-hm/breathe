## Cross-Cutting Concerns

### Root cause: the assistant and entitlement features were built fastest and reviewed least

Four of the five criticals and a disproportionate share of the warnings land in `features/assistant/` and `features/entitlement/`, the two newest surfaces. They are also the two that touch money — one spends it with a third party, the other decides who may. The pattern is consistent: the _mechanisms_ are right (the quota and breaker are server-side, per user, before the provider call; the certificate chain walk is tight and its rejection paths are thoroughly tested), while the _bindings around them_ are loose (a transaction bound to no user, an ordering marker cleared on revocation, no length bound on the submitted JWS). Fixing the mechanism is not what is needed here; fixing what the mechanism is anchored to is.

### Root cause: no `user_id` and no method name reaches any log line

[OBS-001] and [OBS-002] share one cause — no span is ever opened. `identity::resolve` has the `UserId` in hand at the cheapest possible moment and does not record it, and `TraceLayer`'s defaults mean no span exists to attach it to. This is why the five `errors.rs` logs, which are otherwise exemplary (every one logs its server fault before converting and forwards only an opaque `internal` status), produce lines that cannot be attributed to a caller or an RPC. One fix in `identity.rs` plus one constructor change in `lib.rs` resolves both, and makes [OBS-005]'s breaker transitions and [OBS-011]'s token counts worth adding.

### Root cause: the Swift logging surface grew with no document behind it

[OBS-006] (three different subsystem strings), [OBS-007] (20 sites where the error cause renders as `<private>` in any collected log), and [OBS-009] (four degradation paths that record nothing) are all instances of [OBS-017] — `docs/observability.md` covers the backend only, and 14 loggers across three targets have each picked their conventions from whichever neighbour they were copied from. The document is the fix that prevents the next three.

### Systemic: documentation drift is the single largest category

Six findings ([ARCH-001], [ARCH-002], [ARCH-003], [ARCH-007], [ARCH-008], [ARCH-017]) plus [QUAL-009] and [OBS-014] report the same failure — `docs/` describes the repository as it was at the bootstrap, not as it is. The expired "not yet" claims are the dangerous ones: `docs/architecture.md` still says "No auth" after an identity model, a `users` table and three per-person features shipped, and still says there is no deployment target while `infra/` stands up a live box. Two whole surfaces — the `BreatheWatch` app and `web/` — appear in no document at all.

This matters more here than in most repositories, because the doc comments in this codebase are unusually good and readers have no reason to distrust them. `docs/architecture.md` is the file agents and contributors are pointed at first, and two of its five "decisions worth knowing" are now false.

Not everything expired: `docs/observability.md`'s claims all still hold (no metrics, no tracing export, no hand-written retry loop, `/health` and `/about` still the only HTTP routes and both still infallible, so the correlation-id deferral stands), no `shared` crate exists, `AGENTS.md` is still a symlink, and `CLAUDE.md`'s three-environment-variable rule is intact — `config.rs` reads exactly `BREATHE_ENV`, `DATABASE_URL` and `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`.

### Cross-language drift: one contract, two enforcement standards

The Rust side pins `an_unspecified_board_or_scope_is_refused`; the Swift side maps `LeaderboardBoard` and `LeaderboardScope` onto proto with no test ([QUAL-002]). Because both sides are enums of equal arity, any permutation type-checks, and a swapped arm produces a well-formed request the server answers happily — the person sees the wrong board under the right heading. This is the one place where the repo's otherwise-exemplary boundary discipline is one-sided.

The inverse drift is [TYPE-002]: `UserId` exists as a newtype in Rust and is destructured one line into every handler, so ~30 signatures below the transport layer take a bare `Uuid`. `insert_bolt_score(pool, user_id: Uuid, client_score_id: Uuid, …)` takes two adjacent same-typed identifiers positionally; swapping them compiles and the e2e tests, which use one user per test, would still pass.

### Two paths silently truncate the same explanation

[TYPE-001] (a mid-stream model failure ends the stream with `OK` via `map_while`) and [PERF-013] (the client-wide 45-second timeout bounds the streaming body, not just the connect) produce the identical symptom: a truncated explanation the app captions as complete. They are independent causes and both need fixing, but [TYPE-001] is the one that makes the other observable — until the stream terminates with a non-OK status, no client-side signal exists for either.

### Contradictions resolved

- **[ARCH-018] vs [QUAL-006] on phone/watch duplication.** ARCH proposed moving `TechniqueGlyph` into `BreatheKit`; QUAL proposed leaving it and extracting `GoalAccent` instead. **QUAL wins.** The two `TechniqueGlyph` copies have deliberately diverged (line weight, `.fixedSize`), while the two `GoalAccent` copies are byte-identical in their switch bodies — and a divergence there means the same technique is a different colour on the wrist than in the hand, silently. Extract the identical one; leave the deliberately different one.
- **[ARCH-005] vs [OBS-014] on `assistant/service.rs` logging.** Both flagged the same six `tracing::` calls in a service layer. Both concluded the code is right and the document is incomplete: the model error terminates in the service rather than propagating, so the service _is_ the outermost boundary that will ever see it. **Resolved as a documentation finding** — `docs/observability.md` needs a third named pattern, not the code changed.
- **[SEC-012] on the Plus catalogue lock.** The agent correctly declined to file this as a hole. `requires_subscription` is advisory because the session runs on the device, so a server-side gate would guard nothing. It is recorded as a position to hold explicitly, not a defect.

### Dropped as non-findings

Per `docs/testing.md`, no finding was filed against trivial CRUD, ruleless conversions, thin wrappers, or framework behaviour. Notably, "there is no authentication" was correctly not filed by the security agent — it is the documented V1 design, and `users.apple_user_id` is the stated attachment point.

### [ARCH-001] `docs/architecture.md` still claims "No auth" after an identity model shipped

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `docs/architecture.md` (Decisions worth knowing → "No auth")
- **Issue:** The doc defers designing an identity model until "the next feature that stores anything per-person — saved sessions, streaks, HealthKit sync". All three triggers have shipped: `crates/api/src/identity.rs` implements anonymous-UUID middleware, migration `0004_users_and_profiles` added the `users` table, and `profile`, `journey` and `entitlement` are all per-person. The decision has been made and is undocumented at architecture level — the only record is the `//!` block on `identity.rs`.
- **Impact:** The most consequential paragraph in the doc for a new reader says the codebase has no identity concept, when possession of a UUID _is_ the credential, three services require it, and a paid tier hangs off it. Anyone trusting it will design a second mechanism or assume the per-person surface is unprotected.
- **Action:** Replace the section with the actual model: anonymous UUID minted client-side and kept in the Keychain, sent on every RPC, resolved by `identity.rs` as middleware layered inside `GrpcWebLayer` but only on the gRPC router, `TechniqueService` deliberately readable without it, and `users.apple_user_id` reserved for Sign in with Apple. Keep the trade-off sentence — it is the valuable part — but state it as taken, not deferred.

#### Warnings — Type Safety & Robustness

### [TYPE-001] A mid-stream model failure ends the explanation stream with `OK`

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/features/assistant/service.rs:178-189`, `ios/Packages/BreatheCore/Sources/BreatheKit/ExplanationModel.swift:56-76`
- **Issue:** `from_model` uses `map_while`, turning a `ModelError` arriving mid-answer into `None` — the stream simply ends and tonic terminates it with `OK`. `AssistantRepository` correctly throws on a terminal `.complete` carrying a non-OK code, but no non-OK code is ever sent, so that branch never fires. `ExplanationModel` then sets `state = .reading(text:…, isComplete: true)` on whatever arrived. `docs/transport.md` names this exact hazard.
- **Impact:** A provider that dies after two sentences produces an explanation the app presents as finished, mid-sentence, with a "complete" caption. The client, the tests and the reader all see a short answer.
- **Action:** In `from_model`, replace `map_while` with a map that yields `Err(Status::unavailable("the explanation stopped early"))` and then stops — the Swift side already handles that. In `ExplanationModel`, keep the "half an explanation is worth reading" rule but distinguish it, setting `isComplete: false` so the view does not caption a broken stream as finished.

### [TYPE-002] `UserId` is destructured at every handler, so the layers below take bare `Uuid`

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/identity.rs:33-38`; every `let UserId(user_id) = identity::require(&request)?;`; all of `features/{journey,profile,assistant,entitlement}/{service,repository}.rs`
- **Issue:** `identity.rs` defines `UserId(Uuid)` and documents why, then every one of the ten handlers immediately unwraps it — roughly thirty signatures below the transport layer take `user_id: Uuid`. The sharpest case is `journey::repository::insert_bolt_score(pool, user_id: Uuid, client_score_id: Uuid, …)`: two adjacent, positionally-passed `Uuid` parameters naming different concepts.
- **Impact:** The guarantee the newtype exists for is spent one line into every request. A swap in `insert_bolt_score` or `delete_sessions` is a data-attribution bug with no compile-time or test-time signal — the e2e tests use one user per test, so a swapped pair still passes.
- **Action:** Carry `UserId` down through `service.rs` and `repository.rs`, unwrapping only at the `sqlx::query!` argument. Add a `ClientScoreId`/`ClientSessionId` newtype for the adjacent-`Uuid` calls, or at minimum reorder so the two are not adjacent.

### [TYPE-003] The Swift repositories discard the gRPC status code, so a permanent rejection retries forever and reports success

### [OBS-001] No per-request record exists in production

- **Severity:** Critical
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/lib.rs:85`, with `crates/api/src/obs.rs:15-16`
- **Issue:** Two independent defects in one line. `build_app` installs `TraceLayer::new_for_http()` with every default; in tower-http 0.7 `DEFAULT_MESSAGE_LEVEL = Level::DEBUG`, used by `DefaultMakeSpan`, `DefaultOnRequest` and `DefaultOnResponse`. "Which RPC was failing at 14:03" is unanswerable from the log.
- **Severity note:** Filed as Critical rather than the usual Warning for missing observability, because this is a defect in code — the wrong constructor and a level guaranteeing silence — not an omission, and it removes the ability to diagnose a production incident at all.
- **Action:** Configure the layer explicitly: `TraceLayer::new_for_grpc()` (which classifies on `grpc-status`) with `.make_span_with(DefaultMakeSpan::new().level(Level::INFO))` and `.on_response(DefaultOnResponse::new().level(Level::INFO))`, or keep DEBUG and add `tower_http::trace=debug` to the default filter. ### [SEC-002] A signed transaction is a bearer token bound to no user

- **Severity:** Critical
- **Location:** `crates/migrate/migrations/0007_entitlements.sql` (the `app_store_original_transaction_id` column comment), `crates/api/src/features/entitlement/service.rs:23-50`
- **Issue:** `submit_transaction` verifies the JWS, then writes the grant to whichever `breathe-user-id` sent it. Nothing checks whether that `original_transaction_id` is already held by a different row, and the column is explicitly `-- Deliberately not UNIQUE`. The `jwsRepresentation` is a plain string a person can copy off their own device and replay from any gRPC client under any self-minted UUID.
- **Impact:** The migration's stated trade — "roughly $4.99 a year" against a person reinstalling with a new identity — no longer describes the exposure. Under the tier split now landing, the top tier's whole content is the language model and `daily_model_calls(Tier::Coach)` is 25. One leaked or shared JWS therefore grants **25 provider calls per UTC day per identity, with no limit on the number of identities**, all billed to `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`. That defeats the exact control `assistant::service::claim_call` documents itself as being — "the only place a client could otherwise talk the server into spending money". The spend ceiling is per user; the user is free.
- **Action:** Bind the transaction on first claim. Add a `UNIQUE` index on `app_store_original_transaction_id` and, on conflict, either refuse with a distinct status or allow a transfer under an explicit bounded rule — move the grant to the new row, clear the old, cap the transfers, log each one. That preserves the legitimate reinstall case the comment cares about while closing the fan-out. **Do this before Coach ships**: the free-reinstall case costs a fixed subscription price; this one costs per use.

### [SEC-001] A refunded subscription can be restored by replaying the pre-refund transaction **(in-flight)**

- **Severity:** Critical
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/features/entitlement/repository.rs:125-144`, `:70-106`
- **Issue:** `clear_purchase` nulls `subscription_tier`, `subscription_until` and `subscription_signed_at` but deliberately leaves `app_store_original_transaction_id` set. `record_purchase`'s only staleness guard is `AND (subscription_signed_at IS NULL OR subscription_signed_at < $5)`. Immediately after a revocation that guard is `NULL` and therefore open to _any_ signedDate — including the original pre-refund transaction for the same subscription, which the client still holds and which verifies perfectly, because its payload has no `revocationDate`.
- **Impact:** Refunds are undoable by the refunded party, indefinitely, until the transaction's own `expiresDate`. This is reachable by an _honest_ client, not only an attacker: the client resubmits whatever StoreKit hands it on every launch, and `Transaction.updates` and `currentEntitlements` have no ordering between them — which is the reason `signedDate` ordering was introduced in the first place. If the revoked JWS lands before the older non-revoked one in a single launch, the refund silently reverses. No App Store Server Notification path exists to re-assert the revocation.
- **Action:** Do not clear the ordering marker on revocation. Either keep `subscription_signed_at` set to the revoking transaction's `signedDate` so any earlier submission loses the `<` comparison — clearing `app_store_original_transaction_id` only when a newer purchase for a different original id arrives — or record revoked transaction ids and reject a purchase whose `original_transaction_id` is on that list. Add `a_refund_cannot_be_undone_by_resubmitting` to `tests/e2e/entitlement.rs`, beside the existing `resubmitting_the_same_transaction_changes_nothing`.

#### Warnings — Security & Hygiene

### [SEC-003] Any caller can create unbounded `users` rows, on the public catalogue path

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/identity.rs:56-119`
- **Issue:** `resolve` runs on every gRPC request and `ensure_user` does an unconditional `INSERT INTO users (id) … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` for any well-formed UUID in the header. No rate limit exists anywhere in the stack — not in `build_app`, not in `infra/box/Caddyfile`. `ListTechniques` needs no identity, so a loop over random UUIDs inserts a row per request.
- **Impact:** Row-count and disk exhaustion against a `t4g.small` with a 10 GiB EBS data volume, and each new row is a fresh identity with its own daily assistant allowance and its own leaderboard participation. The module doc anticipates the _cost_ of the write but not that the write is attacker-triggered and unbounded. This is also the precondition that makes [PERF-009] an amplification vector.
- **Action:** Add a request-rate limit at the edge — Caddy's `rate_limit` on the `@api` matcher, or `tower_governor` in `build_app` keyed on client IP — before this is publicly reachable. The bounded seen-id set the module doc already proposes as the scaling fix would also cap the insert rate; either works, but something must.

### [SEC-005] `signed_transaction` is accepted with no length bound

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/features/entitlement/service.rs:23-29`, `entitlement/handlers/grpc.rs:30-46`
- **Issue:** The handler passes `signed_transaction` straight to `verifier.verify` with no size check. `service.rs` owns validation per the layering contract and applies none here — unlike `journey::service` (`MAX_SLUG_CHARS`, `MAX_SESSIONS_PER_BATCH`) and `profile::service` (`MAX_INTENT_NOTE_CHARS`), which bound every free-form field. The only ceiling is tonic's default 4 MiB decode limit.
- **Impact:** A 4 MiB "JWS" is split, base64-decoded three times and JSON-parsed before anything rejects it — per request, with no rate limit in front. A real `jwsRepresentation` is a few kilobytes.
- **Action:** Add `MAX_SIGNED_TRANSACTION_BYTES` (8 KiB is generous) in `entitlement/service.rs` and reject over it with `EntitlementError::Invalid` before calling the verifier.

### [SEC-006] Committed Terraform default opens SSH to the entire internet

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `infra/variables.tf` (`admin_cidr`), consumed at `infra/main.tf`
- **Issue:** `default = "0.0.0.0/0"`. The description acknowledges it ("Defaults open because a home IP changes under DHCP") and suggests tightening in `terraform.tfvars` — which is gitignored, so nothing in the repo or CI can tell whether it was. A `tofu apply` with no tfvars stands the box up with 22/tcp world-reachable.
- **Impact:** The instance carries the production database, the Postgres password on the data volume, and an instance profile with `s3:PutObject` on the backup bucket. Key-only auth is the Ubuntu default so this is exposure rather than compromise — but it is exposure the committed default chooses, and the safe default costs nothing.
- **Action:** Drop the default so `tofu plan` fails until an operator names a CIDR, exactly as `ssh_public_key` already does. The `AmazonSSMManagedInstanceCore` attachment already provides the break-glass path the open default was hedging against.

### [SEC-007] gRPC reflection is registered unconditionally and explicitly published in production

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/grpc.rs:29-33`, `infra/box/Caddyfile`
- **Cross-referenced by:** OBS
- **Issue:** `tonic_reflection` is added to `Routes` with no `Environment` check, and the production Caddyfile deliberately proxies `/grpc.reflection.*` alongside the domain services. The doc comment justifies reflection for `grpcurl` convenience, which is a development need. `Config::is_local()` already exists and is used two files away for exactly this class of decision.
- **Impact:** The full service and message schema — every RPC, field and enum, including the entitlement surface — is enumerable by anyone, which removes the work from finding the endpoints in [SEC-002] and [PERF-009]. `docs/observability.md`'s rule that a debug surface must not sit on the public listener applies directly.
- **Action:** Gate registration on `state.config.is_local()` and drop `/grpc.reflection.*` from the Caddyfile matcher — with reflection unregistered, that path falls through to `file_server` and 404s, which is the correct answer. If reflection is wanted against the deployed box, that is the argument for the separate-port pattern the doc already reserves for metrics.

### [SEC-008] Nothing is watching the advisory feed

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `.github/workflows/checks.yml`, `.mise.toml`
- **Issue:** Confirmed still true. CI runs `check:rs`, `check:proto`, `check:text`, `check:md`, `check:doc-links` and `check:swift` — no dependency audit. There is no `.github/dependabot.yml`, no `cargo audit`/`cargo deny` task, and nothing watching `Package.resolved`. `check:deps` is `cargo machete` and answers a different question.
- **Impact:** A published advisory against `ring`, `x509-parser`, `sqlx`, `tonic`, `reqwest` or their transitive trees would go unnoticed indefinitely. `ring` and `x509-parser` are the ones that matter — they are the whole of the App Store signature check.
- **Action:** Add a `check:audit` task running `cargo audit` (or `cargo deny check advisories`) and either put it in the `check` chain or run it on a CI schedule; add `.github/dependabot.yml` for `cargo` and `github-actions`. This finding is about the absent mechanism — no audit was run.

### [SEC-009] `Config` derives `Debug` while holding both credentials

- **Severity:** Warning
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/config.rs:25-37`, `crates/api/src/state.rs:18`
- **Cross-referenced by:** OBS
- **Issue:** `#[derive(Debug, Clone)] pub struct Config` holds `database_url: String` — which carries `${POSTGRES_PASSWORD}` inline per `infra/box/compose.yaml:26` — and `openrouter_api_key: Option<String>`, and the struct is stored whole on `AppState`. Nothing logs it today; `main.rs:28` correctly narrows to `?config.environment`. But `openrouter.rs` goes to real trouble elsewhere to keep the key out of logs ("held here and nowhere else… never logged, never returned in an error"), and the comment at `config.rs:34-35` claims the key "never leaves this process", which the derive quietly makes untrue.
- **Impact:** One `tracing::error!(?config, …)` or one `.context(format!("{config:?}"))` puts the Postgres password and the provider key into the production JSON log stream, permanently, with no compile-time signal.
- **Action:** Replace the derive with a manual `impl Debug` printing `environment` and `port` verbatim, `database_url` as host/database only, and the key as `Some("<redacted>")`/`None`. Add a unit test asserting `format!("{config:?}")` contains neither — that is the check that survives the next editor.

#### Suggestions — Security & Hygiene

### [SEC-010] The chain walk does not check that the intermediate may issue certificates

- **Severity:** Suggestion
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/features/entitlement/verifier/appstore.rs:339-400`
- **Issue:** The walk checks, correctly and tightly: exactly three `x5c` entries, `alg == ES256`, the caller-supplied root ignored in favour of the compiled-in one, Apple's marker OID on both leaf and intermediate, validity at `signedDate`, and two `verify_signature` hops to `APPLE_ROOT_CA_G3`. It does not read `basicConstraints` (`CA:TRUE`, `pathLen`) or `keyUsage` (`keyCertSign`) on the intermediate.
- **Impact:** Low, and bounded by the marker OIDs — an exploit needs Apple to have issued an end-entity certificate carrying `1.2.840.113635.100.6.2.1` directly under the G3 root, which they do not do. Worth closing because this is the one place a signature decides who spends money, and the file's stated reason for hand-rolling was that the community crate carried a `TODO: Implement issuer checking`.
- **Action:** Assert the intermediate's `basic_constraints()` reports `ca == true` and that `key_usage` includes `key_cert_sign`, beside the existing `require_marker` calls. `x509-parser` exposes both; no new dependency. **Land this inside [ARCH-010]'s `chain.rs` extraction** rather than as a separate edit to the 590-line file.

### [SEC-011] The backup bucket lacks the hardening the state bucket has, and holds every bearer id

- **Severity:** Suggestion
- **Location:** `infra/main.tf` (`module "backups"`), compared with `infra/bootstrap/main.tf`
- **Issue:** The bootstrap gives the state bucket explicit versioning, SSE, a public-access block and a `DenyInsecureTransport` policy, with a comment explaining why. The backups bucket gets `bucket_prefix` and a 30-day expiry and relies entirely on module defaults.
- **Impact:** The nightly `pg_dump` contains the complete `users` table — and under this identity model every `users.id` **is** a bearer credential for that person's profile, journey and entitlement. The module's v4 defaults do block public access, so this is defence in depth rather than an open door, but the more sensitive of the two buckets is the less explicitly protected.
- **Action:** Mirror the bootstrap treatment: pass `attach_deny_insecure_transport_policy = true`, explicit `block_public_*`, and enable versioning. Four lines, and it removes the dependency on an upstream module's defaults staying put.

### [SEC-012] The Plus catalogue lock is advisory, and the paid catalogue is one unauthenticated RPC away

- **Severity:** Suggestion
- **Location:** `crates/api/src/features/technique/service.rs:15-47`, `proto/breathe/v1/technique_service.proto`
- **Issue:** `ListTechniques` takes `_request` — no identity, by design — and returns every technique with full stage and phase data regardless of `requires_subscription`. The proto comment's reasoning is sound: a session runs on the device, so a server-side gate would guard nothing, and the flag's zero value is deliberately _unlocked_ so a decode gap never charges someone for what they were promised.
- **Impact:** Not a hole in the security sense — no server-side work is being given away — but worth stating plainly that the entire paid catalogue is one unauthenticated RPC away and `requires_subscription` is advisory. That is a revenue decision, and it should be explicitly held rather than an emergent property of where the flag landed.
- **Action:** No code change recommended. If the catalogue's breadth is ever meant to be genuinely withheld, it has to stop being served — a second RPC returning full detail only to entitled callers, with `ListTechniques` returning name/summary/slug for locked rows. Record the current position in `docs/architecture.md` so the next reviewer does not re-derive it.
